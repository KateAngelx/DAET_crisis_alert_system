CHAPTER 1
BACKGROUND OF THE STUDY
Introduction
Tourism has become one of the major contributors to economic growth and community development in many countries, including the Philippines. It generates employment opportunities, stimulates local businesses, preserves cultural heritage, and promotes sustainable development within local communities. The tourism industry benefits various sectors such as transportation, accommodation, food services, and retail establishments, making it an important driver of local economic progress. As the number of domestic and international tourists continues to increase, local government units (LGUs) are encouraged to provide not only quality tourism services but also safe and secure environments for visitors. Ensuring tourist safety has therefore become an essential component of tourism management, as the success and sustainability of a tourist destination depend greatly on its ability to protect visitors from potential risks and emergencies.
Tourist safety is closely associated with effective crisis communication. Crisis communication refers to the systematic dissemination of timely, accurate, and reliable information before, during, and after emergency situations to reduce uncertainty, guide public behavior, and support coordinated response efforts. In the tourism sector, crisis communication plays a critical role because tourists are often unfamiliar with local geography, emergency procedures, evacuation routes, and government communication channels. During emergencies such as typhoons, earthquakes, flooding, health outbreaks, accidents, or security incidents, delays or inaccuracies in information dissemination may increase confusion, panic, and vulnerability among tourists. Consequently, tourism authorities require efficient communication mechanisms that can rapidly deliver emergency instructions and safety advisories to visitors.
The continuous advancement of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) has significantly transformed the way governments and organizations communicate during emergencies. Modern digital technologies enable faster information dissemination through web applications, mobile platforms, cloud computing, and real-time communication services. ICT has become an important component of disaster risk reduction and emergency management because it improves the speed, accessibility, and reliability of communication between authorities and affected individuals. By utilizing centralized databases, internet-based services, and automated notification systems, emergency information can be delivered efficiently to a large number of recipients while reducing dependence on manual communication methods.
One of the most effective applications of ICT in emergency management is the implementation of Emergency Alert Systems (EAS). An Emergency Alert System is a digital communication platform designed to disseminate urgent notifications and safety instructions to targeted individuals during emergencies. Unlike conventional communication methods that primarily rely on public announcements or social media updates, emergency alert systems utilize multiple communication channels—including Short Message Service (SMS), electronic mail (Email), and web or mobile notifications—to ensure that emergency messages reach recipients through various platforms. Multi-channel communication increases the likelihood that users will receive alerts promptly, even when one communication channel becomes unavailable or inaccessible. These systems also enable authorized personnel to create, categorize, monitor, and archive emergency notifications using centralized administrative platforms, thereby improving communication efficiency and operational coordination.
Despite the growing adoption of digital technologies, many local tourism offices in the Philippines still rely on traditional communication methods, including social media posts, public announcements, local radio broadcasts, and manual coordination with concerned agencies. While these approaches provide general public information, they are not specifically designed to deliver personalized and real-time emergency notifications directly to tourists. As a result, visitors may not immediately receive important safety advisories, particularly if they are unfamiliar with official communication channels or are not actively monitoring online announcements. Furthermore, the absence of a centralized communication platform makes it difficult for tourism authorities to monitor alert dissemination, maintain updated tourist records, and coordinate emergency communication efficiently.
The Municipality of Daet, the capital of Camarines Norte, continues to strengthen its tourism industry through the promotion of beaches, surfing destinations, cultural festivals, historical landmarks, and eco-tourism attractions. These tourism activities attract visitors throughout the year and contribute significantly to the municipality's economic development. However, like many coastal municipalities in the Philippines, Daet remains vulnerable to various natural and human-induced hazards, including typhoons, flooding, storm surges, health emergencies, road accidents, and other public safety incidents. During these situations, timely dissemination of emergency information is essential to ensure the safety of both residents and tourists. At present, there is no dedicated tourism-focused crisis communication platform that allows the Daet Municipal Tourism Office to deliver centralized, real-time emergency notifications directly to registered tourists.
To address this need, this study proposes the development of the Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System, a web-based platform designed to enhance emergency communication within the Municipality of Daet. The proposed system will enable authorized personnel to create and disseminate emergency alerts through multiple communication channels, including SMS, email, and web notifications. It will also provide an administrative dashboard for managing tourist information, classifying alerts by type and severity, monitoring notification and audit history, and facilitating efficient communication during emergencies. By integrating modern ICT solutions into tourism management, the proposed system aims to improve the timeliness, reliability, and effectiveness of emergency information dissemination, enhance tourist safety and preparedness, and support the Municipality of Daet in promoting a safer, more resilient, and sustainable tourism environment.
Project Context
The Municipality of Daet, the capital of Camarines Norte, is recognized as one of the province's primary tourism destinations because of its beaches, historical landmarks, surfing sites, cultural festivals, and eco-tourism attractions. Throughout the year, the municipality welcomes both local and foreign tourists, contributing significantly to the local economy through tourism-related businesses and services. As tourism continues to grow, ensuring the safety and security of visitors has become one of the important responsibilities of the Daet Municipal Tourism Office and other concerned local government agencies.
Emergency situations such as typhoons, heavy rainfall, flooding, earthquakes, health emergencies, road accidents, fires, and public safety incidents may occur without warning. During these situations, tourists require timely and reliable information to help them make informed decisions and follow appropriate safety procedures. However, because tourists are generally unfamiliar with the municipality, they often rely on local authorities for emergency updates and guidance.
At present, emergency information is primarily disseminated through social media announcements, official Facebook pages, local radio broadcasts, public announcements, and coordination with emergency response agencies. Although these communication methods provide important public information, they are not specifically designed to deliver real-time emergency notifications directly to tourists. In many cases, tourists may not immediately receive emergency advisories because they are not actively monitoring official social media pages or local news channels. Furthermore, the absence of a centralized communication platform makes it difficult for tourism authorities to manage tourist information, monitor notification delivery, and ensure that all registered visitors receive emergency alerts promptly.
The increasing demand for digital government services highlights the importance of adopting Information and Communication Technology (ICT) solutions that improve public service delivery and emergency communication. Web-based information systems have become valuable tools for managing data, automating communication processes, and disseminating information efficiently to large groups of users. By integrating multi-channel communication technologies such as SMS, email, and web notifications, emergency information can be distributed quickly and simultaneously through multiple platforms, improving the reliability and effectiveness of crisis communication.
In response to these challenges, this study proposes the development of the Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System, a web-based platform that will serve as a centralized communication system for the Daet Municipal Tourism Office. The system will allow authorized personnel to register tourists, create and publish emergency alerts with type and severity, disseminate notifications through SMS, email, and in-app notifications, and maintain a history of emergency announcements. Through these features, the proposed system aims to strengthen emergency communication, improve tourist preparedness, and support the municipality's efforts in promoting a safer and more resilient tourism environment.
Purpose and Description
The primary purpose of this study is to develop a Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System that will improve the dissemination of emergency information to tourists through a centralized, web-based communication platform. The proposed system aims to assist the Daet Municipal Tourism Office in providing timely, accurate, and reliable emergency notifications during crises such as natural disasters, severe weather conditions, health emergencies, accidents, and other public safety incidents. By utilizing modern information and communication technologies, the system seeks to strengthen crisis communication, enhance tourist preparedness, and support the municipality's commitment to ensuring visitor safety.
The proposed system is designed as a web-based application that enables authorized personnel from the Daet Municipal Tourism Office to create, manage, and distribute emergency alerts to registered tourists and other stakeholders. Through a secure administrative dashboard, administrators can manage user accounts, compose and broadcast emergency announcements, review incident reports, and monitor notification history, and oversee the dissemination of alerts. Tourists, on the other hand, can register their personal and contact information to receive emergency notifications whenever an incident occurs.
To improve the effectiveness of emergency communication, the system integrates multiple communication channels, including Short Message Service (SMS), electronic mail (Email), and web-based notifications. This multi-channel notification approach increases the likelihood that emergency information will reach tourists promptly, regardless of the communication platform they are currently using. The system also maintains a centralized database that stores user information, emergency alerts, and notification records, allowing authorized personnel to efficiently manage communication activities and monitor the status of disseminated alerts.
Unlike conventional communication methods that rely primarily on social media announcements or public advisories, the proposed system provides a dedicated platform specifically designed for tourism-related emergency communication. By centralizing the creation, management, and dissemination of emergency notifications, the system minimizes delays in information delivery, improves coordination among tourism personnel, and ensures that registered tourists receive timely safety advisories during emergency situations.
Ultimately, the proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System is expected to enhance the efficiency of emergency communication within the Municipality of Daet by providing a reliable, organized, and technology-driven solution for crisis information dissemination. The system aims to contribute to improved tourist safety, increased public confidence in local tourism services, and the promotion of a safer, more resilient, and sustainable tourism environment.
Objectives
General Objective:
The general objective of this study is to develop a Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System that enables the Daet Municipal Tourism Office to efficiently disseminate timely and reliable emergency notifications to tourists and tourism guides through a centralized web-based platform.
Specific Objectives:
Specifically, this study aims to:
To analyze the existing emergency communication process of the Daet Municipal Tourism Office and identify the functional and non-functional requirements of the proposed system. 
To design and develop a web-based Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System with features including tourist registration, role-based access for tourists, tourism guides, and administrators, emergency alert management through the Command Center, incident reporting and response tracking, route and hazard advisories, and multi-channel notification through SMS, email, and in-app notifications. 
To implement a centralized database for managing tourist and guide profiles, emergency alerts, incident reports, notification and delivery records, route advisories, and area hazards to improve emergency communication and information management. 
To evaluate the developed system using the Functional Suitability characteristic of the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model. 
Scope and Limitations
Scope:
This study covers the analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation of the Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System (public brand: DAET TOURISM), a web-based platform developed for the Daet Municipal Tourism Office of the Municipality of Daet, Camarines Norte. The proposed system aims to enhance emergency communication by providing a centralized platform for disseminating timely and reliable emergency notifications to registered tourists and tourism guides, while allowing the public to view active official alerts without an account.
The system enables authorized personnel to create, manage, broadcast, and resolve emergency alerts through Short Message Service (SMS), electronic mail (Email), and in-app (web channel) notifications. It includes functionalities such as tourist self-registration, secure user authentication, profile and notification preferences, the public Crisis Hub and resolved-alert archive, the Command Center for administrators, incident reporting with status tracking, route and travel advisories with maps, area hazard warnings, tourism guide dashboards and tour-group assignment, notification and activity history, CSV export of users and incidents, and administrative settings for broadcast audiences and channel testing. Active crisis alerts are visible on the Crisis Hub without login; SMS, email, and in-app delivery target registered users according to profile preferences and office broadcast rules. The system utilizes a centralized Supabase (PostgreSQL) database. Furthermore, the developed system will be evaluated using the Functional Suitability characteristic of the ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model to determine whether it performs its intended functions according to the specified requirements.
Limitations:
This study is limited to the development and evaluation of a web-based crisis communication and emergency alert system. The proposed system does not include disaster prediction, automated environmental sensing, continuous real-time GPS tracking of tourists, artificial intelligence-based decision support, or automatic emergency response dispatch. Map features show office-published alert, route, and hazard locations but do not track devices in real time. It serves solely as a communication and information dissemination platform and does not replace the functions and responsibilities of emergency response agencies such as the Municipal Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Office (MDRRMO), Philippine National Police (PNP), Bureau of Fire Protection (BFP), or emergency medical services.
Since the system is web-based, it requires a stable internet connection for both administrators and registered users to access its features. Without internet connectivity, users cannot register, log in, receive in-app notifications, submit incident reports, or access the full web application. Likewise, administrators cannot create or broadcast emergency alerts, manage user accounts, or update incident report status while offline.
The delivery of SMS notifications depends on the availability of mobile network services and the SMS gateway provider. Registered users in areas with weak or no cellular signal may experience delays or may not receive SMS notifications until network connectivity is restored. Similarly, email notifications require an active internet connection and the availability of email service providers.
Incident report submission and in-app notification delivery also depend on internet connectivity. Reports and status updates may not be processed immediately during connection problems, power interruptions, server downtime, or hosting service interruptions. Processing resumes once connectivity and system services are restored.
SMS and email notifications are sent only to registered users with valid contact information; SMS delivery additionally depends on user SMS preferences, the iProg SMS gateway, and mobile network coverage. In-app notifications require users to be signed in to the web application. The findings of this study are limited to the selected respondents and the testing environment used during the evaluation and may not necessarily represent the system's performance in all deployment environments.

Administrator – Refers to the authorized personnel of the Daet Municipal Tourism Office responsible for managing user accounts, issuing and broadcasting emergency alerts through the Command Center, reviewing and updating incident reports, and maintaining the overall operation of the system.
Alert Type and Severity – Refers to the classification of official crisis alerts (for example, typhoon, flooding, health advisory) together with severity levels (such as Critical, High, Medium, Low) assigned when an administrator publishes an alert.
Centralized Database – Refers to the Supabase (PostgreSQL) repository used to store and manage user profiles, emergency alerts, incident reports, notification and delivery records, route advisories, area hazards, and related tourism data in a single database.
Crisis Communication – Refers to the process of delivering timely, accurate, and reliable emergency information to tourists before, during, and after crisis situations through the proposed system.
Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System (DAET TOURISM) – Refers to the web-based information system developed in this study that enables the Daet Municipal Tourism Office to publish crisis alerts, manage tourist and guide accounts, broadcast multi-channel notifications, and process incident reports from registered tourists.
Email Notification – Refers to an electronic message automatically sent by the system to the registered email addresses of users to inform them of emergency situations and safety advisories.
Emergency Alert – Refers to an official crisis notification created by authorized administrators that appears on the public Crisis Hub when marked public and active, and may trigger SMS, email, and in-app notifications to registered users when broadcast.
Functional Suitability – Refers to the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality characteristic used in this study to evaluate whether the developed system provides the appropriate functions that meet the specified requirements and intended purpose.
ISO/IEC 25010 Software Quality Model – Refers to the international software quality standard used in this study as the basis for evaluating the Functional Suitability of the developed system.
Notification History – Refers to the system-generated record of previously disseminated emergency alerts and notifications for monitoring, tracking, and future reference.
Registered Tourist – Refers to a tourist who has successfully created an account in the system by providing the required registration information, including a valid email address and mobile phone number, enabling the user to receive emergency notifications and access the system's services.
Short Message Service (SMS) – Refers to the mobile text messaging service integrated into the proposed system to deliver emergency notifications directly to the registered mobile phone numbers of users.
User Authentication – Refers to the security process used by the system to verify the identity of registered users and administrators before granting access to the system.
Incident Report – Refers to the feature that allows registered tourists to submit hazard or emergency reports with category, severity, and location details, and to track tourism office response status through the system.
In-App Notification – Refers to a notification record delivered inside the signed-in DAET TOURISM application (Notifications inbox and on-screen alerts), distinct from SMS and email channels.
Tourism Guide – Refers to a user assigned the guide role by the Daet Municipal Tourism Office who can monitor crisis alerts, assigned tour groups, and tourists linked to their groups during an incident.
Command Center – Refers to the administrative module where authorized personnel create, broadcast, update, and resolve official crisis alerts and review related operational activity.
Crisis Hub – Refers to the public-facing section of DAET TOURISM where active and resolved official crisis alerts, maps, and safety instructions are displayed.
Tourism Guide – Refers to a user assigned the guide role by the Daet Municipal Tourism Office who can monitor crisis alerts, assigned tour groups, and tourists linked to their groups during an incident.

CHAPTER 2
REVIEW RELATED LITERATURE AND STUDIES
This chapter presents the review of related literature and studies that serve as the theoretical, conceptual, and technical foundation of the proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System. The reviewed literature discusses concepts related to crisis communication, information and communication technology (ICT), emergency alert systems, tourism safety, and disaster management. Meanwhile, the related studies present existing systems and technological solutions that demonstrate the effectiveness of digital platforms in improving emergency communication and information dissemination. These references provide valuable insights into the design, development, and evaluation of the proposed system while identifying research gaps that justify its implementation.
Review of Related Literature
Crisis communication, disaster management technologies, and emergency alert systems serve as the theoretical and conceptual foundations of this study. Effective crisis communication enables organizations and government agencies to disseminate timely, accurate, and reliable information that minimizes confusion, reduces risks, and enhances public trust during emergencies. With the continuous advancement of Information and Communication Technology, digital communication platforms have become essential tools for improving disaster preparedness, emergency response, and tourist safety. The following foreign and local literature provide significant concepts that support the development of the proposed system.
Foreign Literature
Coombs and Holladay (2022), in The Handbook of Crisis Communication, emphasized that effective crisis response depends on timely, accurate, and multi-channel dissemination of information. The authors introduced the Situational Crisis Communication Theory (SCCT), which explains that organizations should adopt appropriate communication strategies based on the level of crisis responsibility and stakeholder perception. This theory supports the proposed system by emphasizing the importance of delivering reliable emergency information through multiple communication channels to maintain public trust.
Arif and Yu (2026) explained that digital crisis communication has become increasingly important in the hospitality and tourism industry. Their study found that timely digital communication, empathy, and proper risk messaging significantly influence tourists' emotions, confidence, and trust in tourism authorities during crisis situations. These findings support the integration of digital communication features in the proposed emergency alert system.
Similarly, Verma, Dominguez, and Petrov (2025) highlighted that transparent and timely dissemination of emergency information through digital platforms reduces uncertainty and helps maintain tourist confidence during disasters. Their study emphasized that tourism organizations should establish effective communication strategies to ensure visitor safety before, during, and after emergency situations.
Sacyaten (2024) conducted a systematic review of twenty-nine studies on crisis communication during disasters and concluded that the clarity, accuracy, and timeliness of emergency announcements significantly reduce public confusion and improve trust in government authorities. This finding reinforces the need for a centralized emergency communication platform capable of delivering consistent and reliable information.
Permana et al. (2025) investigated mobile disaster preparedness applications and found that push notifications and location-based alerts are among the most effective features in improving user responsiveness and encouraging community participation during emergencies. Although the proposed study does not incorporate continuous real-time GPS tracking of tourists, it provides map-based display of affected areas, route advisories, and hazard locations. The effectiveness of digital notifications supports the use of SMS, email, and in-app notifications for emergency communication.
Local Literature
Lamentillo (2022) discussed the importance of Information and Communication Technology (ICT) in strengthening disaster risk reduction efforts in the Philippines. The study highlighted the use of satellite communication systems and the Government Emergency Communications System in restoring communication during Typhoon Paeng. This demonstrates the importance of reliable communication technologies in emergency management.
Siar and Lorenzo (2022) examined the crisis communication capacity of Philippine Local Government Units (LGUs) and identified inadequate ICT infrastructure and the limited use of digital communication platforms as major challenges affecting emergency communication. Their findings indicate the need for local governments to adopt more efficient digital communication systems to improve public safety.
Jocson (2022) emphasized that digital governance promotes transparency, citizen participation, and improved public service delivery through modern communication platforms. The study recommended that local government units strengthen their digital communication strategies to improve information dissemination and public engagement.
Cabaguing (2024) highlighted that tourism establishments should establish structured crisis readiness and response strategies to ensure that guests receive timely and reliable information during natural disasters and public health emergencies. The study reinforces the significance of effective communication systems in maintaining tourist safety.
Soriano (2023) found that mobile technologies, SMS broadcasting, and web-based communication platforms significantly improve early warning dissemination and disaster response among Philippine local government units. These technologies are directly related to the proposed system's objective of delivering emergency notifications through multiple communication channels.
Review of Related Studies
Existing foreign and local studies demonstrate that digital technologies significantly improve emergency communication, disaster preparedness, and coordination among emergency responders. These studies provide practical evidence supporting the development of centralized emergency communication platforms.
Foreign Studies
Marzouk et al. (2022) developed a multi-channel disaster communication system integrating mobile alerts, email notifications, and web dashboards. Their findings revealed significant improvements in emergency information dissemination and coordination among response teams. The study supports the proposed integration of multiple communication channels within a centralized platform.
Shriharshan, Patel, and Singh (2025) developed an Internet of Things (IoT)-based emergency alert system utilizing environmental sensors and GPS technologies for automatic hazard detection and situational awareness. Although the proposed study does not incorporate IoT sensors, the research demonstrates the importance of technology-driven emergency communication systems.
Nwufoh, Okeke, and Adeyemi (2022) developed a web-based emergency alert management system featuring an administrative dashboard for monitoring emergencies and generating reports. Evaluation results indicated improved operational efficiency and communication management. This study supports the development of a centralized administrative dashboard for the proposed system.
Zou and Yu (2022) investigated the relationship between tourism safety perception and digital crisis communication. Their findings showed that real-time information sharing and transparency significantly increase tourists' confidence and perceived safety during emergencies, reinforcing the importance of timely emergency notifications.
Lile, Petrescu, and Popescu (2025) concluded that ICT-enabled disaster preparedness platforms strengthen stakeholder collaboration, improve emergency planning, and enhance community resilience. Their findings support the implementation of integrated digital communication systems for disaster preparedness.
Local Studies
Rey, Santos, and Rivera (2024) developed Mamamayan, a mobile emergency reporting system utilizing geolocation to identify incident locations. The system achieved a high level of functional acceptance and demonstrated users' willingness to adopt digital emergency reporting technologies.
Goh, Ramirez, and Torres (2023) developed iAlerto, a web and mobile emergency alert system for Pasig City integrating GPS services and evacuation center management. Evaluation results indicated high functional acceptability, demonstrating the effectiveness of web-based emergency communication systems.
Agoylo, Dela Cruz, and Bautista (2024) developed the Monitoring and Emergency Reporting System (MERS), which utilizes centralized information sharing to improve coordination among emergency responders. Their study showed that centralized databases contribute to faster response times and improved operational efficiency.
Gatchalian, Flores, and Tan (2023) developed an SMS-based disaster alert system for rural communities in the Philippines. Their findings demonstrated that SMS notifications remain highly effective in disseminating emergency information, particularly in areas with limited internet connectivity. This supports the inclusion of SMS notifications in the proposed system.
Villanueva, Cruz, and Ramos (2023) developed a web-based disaster notification and monitoring system for municipal emergency response. Simulation results indicated improved dissemination speed and responder coordination, highlighting the effectiveness of centralized web-based emergency communication platforms.
Synthesis
The reviewed foreign and local literature consistently emphasizes that effective crisis communication depends on timely, accurate, and reliable dissemination of information. The integration of Information and Communication Technology (ICT), including web-based platforms, SMS, email, and digital communication tools, has significantly enhanced emergency preparedness, disaster response, and public safety. Likewise, the reviewed studies demonstrate that centralized emergency alert systems improve communication efficiency, coordination among stakeholders, and operational effectiveness during emergencies.
Despite these advancements, most existing systems focus on general disaster management, emergency reporting, or community-based disaster response. Only a few studies specifically address the tourism sector, and none focus on developing a centralized crisis communication and emergency alert system for the Municipality of Daet. Furthermore, many existing systems rely on a single communication platform or emphasize emergency reporting rather than the dissemination of real-time emergency information to tourists.
These identified gaps justify the development of the proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System. The proposed system seeks to provide a centralized web-based platform that enables the Daet Municipal Tourism Office to disseminate emergency alerts through SMS, email, and in-app notifications while supporting incident reporting, guide monitoring of assigned tourists, and multi-channel alert delivery. By addressing the limitations of existing systems, the proposed study aims to enhance tourist safety, improve emergency communication, and support more effective crisis management within the Municipality of Daet.

Analysis
Table 1 shows that the reviewed systems provide various functionalities related to emergency communication, such as emergency alert management, web-based platforms, SMS notifications, and administrative dashboards. However, these systems are generally intended for disaster management, emergency reporting, or public safety and are not specifically designed to address the communication needs of the tourism sector.
The proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System distinguishes itself by integrating multi-channel notifications (SMS, email, and in-app notifications), emergency alert management, an administrative dashboard, and incident reporting within a single web-based platform specifically designed for the Daet Municipal Tourism Office, registered tourists, and tourism guides. This combination of features addresses the limitations of existing systems and fills the identified gap by providing a centralized, tourism-focused emergency communication platform that enhances visitor safety and supports effective crisis communication.
Research Gap
The reviewed foreign and local literature and studies demonstrate that information and communication technologies have significantly improved disaster preparedness, emergency response, and crisis communication through the use of web-based platforms, mobile applications, SMS notifications, and centralized monitoring systems. Several existing systems effectively provide emergency alerts, incident reporting, and disaster monitoring; however, they primarily focus on general disaster management and public emergency response.
Despite these advancements, there remains a significant gap in the development of a tourism-specific crisis communication and emergency alert system tailored to the needs of local government tourism offices. Most related systems lack a centralized platform that integrates multi-channel notifications through SMS, email, and web notifications while also supporting two-way communication between tourism authorities and registered tourists. Furthermore, existing studies seldom emphasize the management of tourists during emergencies or provide features specifically designed to improve visitor safety and communication within tourism destinations.
The proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System addresses these identified gaps by developing a centralized web-based platform specifically intended for the Daet Municipal Tourism Office. The system integrates emergency alert management, multi-channel notification services, an administrative dashboard, and incident reporting with status tracking to facilitate structured communication between tourism personnel and registered tourists. By focusing on the tourism sector, the proposed study contributes to enhancing emergency preparedness, improving information dissemination, and promoting visitor safety during crisis situations.

CHAPTER 3TECHNICAL BACKGROUND
This chapter presents the technical background of the proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System. It describes the research methodology and software development methodology employed in designing, developing, and implementing the proposed system. This chapter also discusses the methods used to gather the necessary information, analyze user requirements, and transform these requirements into a functional web-based emergency communication platform.
Furthermore, this chapter explains the Agile Scrum software development methodology adopted by the researchers, including the product backlog, sprint planning, sprint backlog, sprint development, sprint review, and sprint retrospective. These methodologies provide a systematic approach to the development process, ensuring that the proposed system is developed efficiently, meets user requirements, and delivers the expected functionalities. The technical design and implementation discussed in this chapter serve as the foundation for developing a reliable, secure, and user-friendly emergency communication system for the Daet Municipal Tourism Office.
Research Methodology
The research methodology describes the methods and procedures employed in conducting the study and developing the proposed system. It includes the research method, data gathering procedures, and software development methodology used throughout the project. These methodologies provide a structured framework for identifying user requirements, designing the proposed system, developing its functionalities, and evaluating its effectiveness.
Research Method
This study employed the Descriptive-Developmental Research Method. The descriptive aspect of the study was utilized to identify and analyze the existing communication practices, challenges, and emergency response procedures of the Daet Municipal Tourism Office. Through observation, interviews, and document analysis, the researchers gathered relevant information regarding the current methods of disseminating emergency information to tourists and identified areas requiring technological improvement.
The developmental aspect focused on the design, development, implementation, and evaluation of the proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System. Based on the collected data and identified requirements, the researchers developed a web-based application capable of providing centralized emergency communication through SMS notifications, email notifications, and web-based alerts.
The Descriptive-Developmental Research Method was selected because it enables the researchers to examine the existing organizational processes while simultaneously developing an information system that addresses the identified problems. This method is widely used in information technology research, particularly in software development projects where existing operational procedures are analyzed to develop technological solutions.
Data Gathering Procedure
The researchers employed several data gathering techniques to obtain accurate and reliable information necessary for the development of the proposed system. These techniques ensured that the system requirements reflected the actual needs of the Daet Municipal Tourism Office and its stakeholders.
Interview:
Interviews were conducted with authorized personnel of the Daet Municipal Tourism Office to gather information regarding the current emergency communication procedures, existing challenges, and desired functionalities of the proposed system. The interviews also provided insights into the operational workflow and notification processes used during emergency situations.
Observation:
The researchers observed the existing communication and information dissemination processes within the Daet Municipal Tourism Office. This allowed the researchers to identify inefficiencies in the current system and determine opportunities where technology could improve emergency response and communication.
Document Review:
Relevant documents such as emergency response protocols, tourism safety guidelines, communication procedures, and organizational records were reviewed to support the requirements gathering process. The review provided additional information regarding policies and operational standards related to emergency communication.
Requirements Analysis:
The information collected through interviews, observations, and document reviews was analyzed to determine the functional and non-functional requirements of the proposed system. The identified requirements served as the basis for designing the system architecture, database structure, user interface, and notification modules.
Software Development Methodology
Agile Scrum Methodology
The development of the proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System followed the Agile Scrum Software Development Methodology. Agile Scrum is an iterative and incremental software development framework that emphasizes collaboration, flexibility, and continuous improvement throughout the software development life cycle. Instead of completing the entire system at once, Agile Scrum divides the project into several short development cycles called sprints, where each sprint focuses on delivering a functional component of the system.
The researchers selected Agile Scrum because it allows continuous evaluation and refinement of the developed system based on user feedback and testing results. This methodology enables the development team to adapt to changes in requirements while ensuring that every completed sprint contributes to the overall functionality of the proposed system. Through iterative development, Agile Scrum minimizes project risks, enhances software quality, and ensures that the final product satisfies the functional requirements of the Daet Municipal Tourism Office.
The Agile Scrum methodology consists of several phases, namely the Product Backlog, Sprint Planning, Sprint Development, Sprint Review, and Sprint Retrospective. Each phase contributes to the systematic development of the proposed system.
Product Backlog
The Product Backlog is a prioritized list of all functional requirements and features identified during the requirements gathering phase. It serves as the primary source of work items that will be implemented throughout the development process. The Product Backlog is continuously updated whenever new requirements or improvements are identified.

Sprint Planning
Sprint Planning is conducted at the beginning of every sprint to determine which Product Backlog items will be completed during the sprint. During this phase, the researchers estimated the development effort, prioritized the selected features, identified task dependencies, and assigned responsibilities among the development team members. The objective of Sprint Planning is to ensure that each sprint has a clear goal and a realistic workload that can be completed within the specified time frame.

Sprint Development
Sprint Development is the implementation phase where the selected Sprint Backlog items are designed, coded, integrated, and tested. During this phase, the researchers continuously monitored the development progress and ensured that each completed functionality complied with the identified system requirements. Unit testing and integration testing were also performed to verify the correctness and reliability of each developed module before proceeding to the next sprint.
Sprint Review
At the end of each sprint, the researchers conducted a Sprint Review to evaluate the completed functionalities. The developed modules were demonstrated and assessed to determine whether they satisfied the objectives defined during Sprint Planning. Feedback from advisers and stakeholders was collected and documented for incorporation into subsequent development iterations. This process ensured that the proposed system continually evolved according to user needs and project objectives.
Sprint Retrospective
After each Sprint Review, the researchers conducted a Sprint Retrospective to evaluate the overall performance of the development process. The team discussed the accomplishments, challenges encountered, and possible improvements for future sprints. Lessons learned during each sprint were used to refine development practices, improve collaboration, and enhance the efficiency of succeeding iterations. This continuous improvement process contributed to the successful completion of the proposed Daet Tourism Crisis Communication and Emergency Alert System while maintaining software quality and meeting project objectives.
